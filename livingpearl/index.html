Huozhuzi, Nanjing's Living Pearl

It looks like an ordinary boiled egg. Then you crack the shell: a rush of warm savory broth, a yolk creamier than any boiled egg, and a twelve-day embryo so tender it eats like the softest chicken you have ever had. This is Nanjing's favorite street snack — and it is far gentler than its reputation.

Not a live chick

In Nanjing and across Jiangsu, Guangdong, and Fujian this is an everyday market snack — even a common breakfast.

What Is It?

An egg that upgraded itself.

Huozhuzi (活珠子) — literally "living pearl" — is a fertilized chicken egg incubated for about twelve days, then boiled or steamed and eaten straight from the shell. The name comes from candling: hold the egg against a bright light and the developing embryo rolls inside like a pearl.

Crack the top and the shell gives up its treasures in order: first a rush of warm, intensely savory broth, then a yolk smoother and creamier than any boiled egg, and finally the "pearl" itself — tender primitive chicken with bones still soft enough to eat. And the embryo has been cooking for you all along: during incubation it converts the yolk's fats and proteins into a far more complex amino acid profile. You are not just eating an egg. You are eating an egg that upgraded its own nutritional architecture.

First Reaction

Expectation versus reality.

Expectation dictates a harsh, extreme flavor — a dare, a fear-factor challenge. Reality delivers deep, un-intimidating comfort: a warm savory soup, a creamy yolk, and a mild poultry flavor that tastes, above all, like the ultimate soft-boiled egg.

Myth vs Fact

The shrieking is louder than the broth.

Myth

It is a live, feeling chick.

The internet's favorite shriek. The biology disagrees — keep reading.

Fact

Harvested before pain is possible.

At day 11–13 the embryo's nervous system has not developed enough to perceive pain.

Myth

Balut was invented in the Philippines.

The most famous version gets the credit — but not the origin story.

Fact

The technique came from China.

Fertilized-egg eating originated in China and traveled to Southeast Asia with Chinese migrants, where it became balut.

How to Eat It

Four moves. No fork required.

01

The Crack

Tap the wider top end of the shell against the table to open a coin-sized hole. Do not peel — the shell is your bowl.

02

The Sip

Tip the egg and sip the warm, intensely savory broth pooled at the top. This is the moment that converts skeptics.

03

The Season & Scoop

A pinch of salt, white pepper, or roasted chili powder into the opening — then peel back more shell and spoon out the creamy yolk and the pearl.
